# Changelog

## 1.0.0

First release in Libre Arcade, promoted from the restoration package 1.0.0 after a check against the original ROM and in real browsers.

- Card-matching game with the rules, the random deal, the frame timing, the CPU player, the pad model, the story and the text of Concentration Room 0.02 (Damian Yerrick, 2010, for the Nintendo Entertainment System), ported from the 6502 assembly. Four modes (Story, Solitaire, 2 Players, Vs. CPU), five table sizes from 10 to 72 cards, English and Italian, mouse, touch and the two-pad keyboard, sound synthesised in the browser. No original image is redistributed except the two tile atlases the game draws from.
- Preserved the original under `reference/ConcentrationRoom-0.02/`: the 33 text and data files of the source archive and the release ROM, unmodified, with a checksum list, a manifest of all 39 files of the archive, and a record of where the archive comes from and what is not kept (`SOURCE.md`). The zip and the three illustrations of the documentation are not kept (`reference/README.md`). The preserved files are byte-exact (`.gitattributes`), and `test/reference.test.js` checks it.
- Added an executable oracle: a 6502 core with cycle counts (151 documented opcodes, tested exhaustively for the arithmetic), a minimal NES (memory, controller ports, vblank and the NMI) and `tools/rom-oracle.mjs`, which runs the preserved release ROM from reset, plays it with a scripted player and compares the port's state with the ROM's memory at every iteration of the original's main loop. The recording covers 64 scenarios, 128 games and 310,756 iterations: Solitaire, 2 Players, Vs. CPU and the first four stages of the Story, at all five table sizes, with seeded random players and with players that know the board, including sessions of several games in a row. No difference remains. The addresses of the ROM's variables are found by elimination, 173 by the data and one (the CPU's memory of a card that exists only on the largest table, which the CPU never remembered in these games) by the layout of its array. `npm test` runs six quick scenarios against the ROM again (about ten seconds); `npm run test:live` runs all 64. Thirty deliberate faults put into the port one at a time were caught 28 times (see `specs/port-map.md`).
- Fixed what the ROM showed to differ from the text of the source, which the package had been written from (the release was built with the author's tool run in reverse order, see `specs/port-map.md`): the four directions are handled in the order Right, Down, Left, Up and only Right is skipped while a controller is being handed over (the package skipped all four); the human pad keeps moving the cursor, and B keeps working, while the CPU plays; the CPU's turn discards pad 1's new presses; in Vs. CPU the turn goes to the CPU at once after a miss, without waiting for A and B; a cleared table runs the Still handler on its last frame (so A and B are read there); the state timer is left at 255 after a game, as the ROM leaves it. Most of these cannot be seen in ordinary play.
- Added tests that compare the port's data with the ROM's bytes (`test/rom-data.test.js`): the 90 lines of English text, the ten-stage story table, the eleven sound effects, the five music patterns, the two conductors, the instrument table, the five layouts of the table and the palettes of the cards and the table.
- Fixed what the page draws, found by comparing it with the ROM's own pictures (new: the ROM's frame is composed from the machine's memory and the page's renderers are run in Node over a software canvas, `tools/nes-frame.mjs`, `tools/canvas-shim.mjs`, `tools/render-oracle.mjs`): the open cards' emblems sat above the cards because the background was not scrolled up by the 12 pixels that the ROM scrolls it; the lower two tiles of a card's back and of every emblem were swapped (the emblems had a hole); the arrow, the card that turns and the "Press A+B" message had their colours one place off; the corners of the table were square; the card that turns was a line too high; the second collected card was under the first where they overlap; sprites were drawn under the score boxes and the game-over wipe; the turn marker and the WIN mark came a frame early; the arrow did not park under the new player's score box during a hand-over, nor start each game at its start position; the opponent scenes' text was single spaced (the original double spaces it) and their characters, like the Hombon logo, a line too high; the opening and instruction text was white and single spaced, scrolled for half the time and faded smoothly (it is black, double spaced, scrolls twice as long and fades in three palette steps); the title's cover for the original's notice reached 8 pixels into the black band. The game screen now matches the ROM's in 119,426 frames of the 64 scenarios, the title's two menus (with the arrow on every line), 14 opponent scenes and the opening and instruction texts (all three of them) at 14 moments, pixel by pixel.
- The pictures of the opponents, the Hombon logo and the title are now made from the ROM (`tools/render-assets.mjs`); the package's Python scripts (which could not be run here) and its reference board image (`test/fixtures/board-oracle.png`, drawn with the same errors) were removed. New tests: `test/render-oracle.test.js`, `test/render-title.test.js`, `test/render-story.test.js`; `npm run test:render` compares all 64 scenarios.
- Fixed the audio data: the last two notes of the intro's bass line were quarter notes where the ROM has dotted quarters (found by the data test above).
- Fixed the Help and Credits dialogs on short windows: the card opened already scrolled to its end, because the focus went to the Close button at its foot; it now opens at the top.
- The credits say "HTML5 port" instead of "HTML5 restoration and preservation", and "Versione HTML5" in Italian.
- Checked in real browsers: no scroll in either direction, a canvas of the NES's proportions and no control outside the window at 14 window sizes in English and in Italian (320 by 568 to 1920 by 1080, landscape from 568 by 320), in the title, both dialogs, the difficulty menu, the instruction scene and a game with two cards turned; and games played with real mouse and keyboard events: a Solitaire game won and one lost on its last miss (back to the title each time), a game for two with a miss, the Continue button and a win by the second player, a miss against the CPU and the CPU taking its turn, the first stage of the Story, language and difficulty remembered across a reload, the Help dialog (background inert, Escape closes, focus returns, a click outside closes), the Credits links, the reset of the saved preferences, the audio switch with sound playing, and a resize during a game with a click landing on the right card afterwards. No console error and no failed request.
- Joined the Libre Arcade collection: the page at the root of `public/`, `npm test` runs `node --test`, and the collection scaffolding was added (`scripts/serve.mjs`, `scripts/build.mjs`, an ESLint config, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, the GoatCounter snippet, the "Play here" link, the provenance, archaeology and port-map documents, the story). The delivered tests moved to `public/src/` paths, the Python asset scripts read the preserved sources and write to the new places, an unused import and two unused helpers were removed to satisfy the linter.
- Kept the package's development notes, test report and manifest, with their old paths, unmodified in `docs/development/`.

**Known limits.** On very large windows the game frame stops growing at 900 by 760 pixels, so the picture tops out at twice the console's size (512 by 480). The sound approximates the console's sound chip; its notes and timings are the original's. Nothing here plays sound, so the playback was not compared with anything; the pictures were compared for the game screen, the title menus, the opponent scenes and the opening and instruction text, and not for the page's own fades and overlay, or the colours that the original spoils at the end of the last opponent's lost text (the page keeps that text and not the spoiled colours). The Select-button slowdown of the original (fifteen times slower while held) is not offered. The Story is checked against the ROM for its first four stages; the remaining six are checked by their table and by the same state machine. The differences that were found by reading the original and not changed are in `specs/port-map.md`.
