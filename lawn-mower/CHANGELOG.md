# Changelog

## 1.0.0

First release in Libre Arcade, promoted from the restoration package `Lawn_Mower_M9_RC6` after a check against the competition ROM, the author's own v1.12 ROM and the v1.12 source run on a small NES of our own, and in real browsers.

- A mowing game with the ten levels, the movement, the fuel, the cans, the flowers and the stones, the messages, the final screen, the music and the sound effects of Lawn Mower (Shiru, 2011, for the Nintendo Entertainment System), ported from the 6502 assembly of the v1.12 source. English and Italian, keyboard and touch, sound that can be switched off. Its hidden cheat is in it (see the README).
- Preserved the original under `reference/`: the competition ROM (v1.1) with the author's entry form and notes, the author's own build of v1.12 (from the Internet Archive's lawn_mower.zip, downloaded and checked against the item's metadata; the zip is not the competition archive), and the assembler source of v1.12 with the three binary inputs of its assembly and the level editor's sources and level file, all unmodified, with checksum lists, manifests of the three archives and a record of where each comes from (the source archive is byte for byte the Internet Archive's) and what is not kept (`reference/README.md`). The zips, the editor program, the tracker projects, the manual and the label are not kept.
- Added an executable oracle (`tools/`): a 6502 core with cycle counts, a minimal NES (memory, controller ports, the vertical blank and the NMI, the scroll registers, the sprite 0 hit, a log of register writes), an assembler for the source (`tools/assemble.mjs`, ported from the package's Python one; it reproduces the package's two verification ROMs byte for byte and is not the author's binary), a scanline compositor that draws the ROM's picture from its memories (`tools/nes-frame.mjs`), players that read the ROM's memory to mow, fetch fuel and press things at random, and `tools/lockstep.mjs`, which runs the ROM and the port side by side and compares, at every NMI, the game's variables, the picture unit's memories, the sound player's output and, with `tools/oracle.mjs`, the picture pixel by pixel. Run against the competition ROM, the author's v1.12 ROM and the assembled source (for the author's v1.12 ROM the port follows the ROM's length for each screen set-up, because the loading of the fifth lawn takes 10 NMIs there and 9 in the other two; nothing else differs); `npm test` runs it shortly and `npm run test:live` long. See `specs/port-map.md` and `PROVENANCE.md`.
- Fixed what the comparison showed to differ from the original, in the package's port:
  - the game logic: a skipped NMI runs the game anyway when the fuel routine goes looking for a place for the next can (it leaves X not zero); the borders and the clearing of the lawn's map; the tile check at the start of a level and the two coordinates a level keeps; the pad is read only in some loops, with the original's "new press" rule, and un-pausing and the final screen need START to be the only new button; the cheat (RIGHT, UP, LEFT, LEFT, then SELECT) was missing; the frames the display is off between screens (10, 9, 1 or 2) count in the frame counter; the messages' map, the blanking of their columns on skipped NMIs, the sprites taken off the screen, the rows redrawn while they wait; the level counter reaches 10 on the final screen; the cheat's buffer starts again with each showing of the title;
  - the picture: the original's sprite priority (the mower's body had lost its details because the package painted the sprites in table order), the eight sprites to a line, the wrap of the left-edge sprites, the fade through the original's brightness table applied to half the palette on each NMI (the package laid black over the picture), the pause as a step of that fade, the message's flash band at lines 118 to 155 (the package's was two lines higher), the animation byte of the message;
  - the sound: a silent channel keeps its last pitch in the output buffer, idle effect streams are not mixed in, the mute is the effect number 10 mixed once, the clear's mute comes ten counts after the tune stops, the can's sound is heard in the NMI that adds it, the cheat plays the skip sound, the music's start duty.
- The picture is now made from the picture unit's memories (`public/src/ppu.js`: nametables, palette, sprite memory, with the original's routines for tiles, numbers and the fade) by `public/src/display.js`, band by band where the original changes the scroll; the package's renderer (`render-core.js`, `canvas-renderer.js`, `ppu-timing.js`) was removed. The page runs one NMI of the game per step (`session.js`) and gives the sound player the events in the order the original does.
- Added tests: the tables, the ten levels and the two screens against the ROM's bytes (`test/rom-data.test.js`); the music and effects data against the source's export files, assembled again (`test/famitone-data.test.js`); the assembler (`test/assemble.test.js`); the machine and the compositor (`test/nes-machine.test.js`, `test/mos6502.test.js`); the three oracle runs (`test/oracle-v1.1.test.js`, `test/oracle-release-v1.12.test.js`, `test/oracle-v1.12.test.js`); thirteen deliberate breaks of the port that the comparison must see (`test/oracle-sensitivity.test.js`); properties of the game logic (`test/kernel.test.js`); the preserved files (`test/reference.test.js`); the page (`test/page.test.js`). The package's tests, which compared the port with its own output, were not kept.
- The one place where the competition ROM and the v1.12 source differ in what the game does is kept as a switch of the game logic (`checkPadClearsDirection`); the page follows v1.12. A test finishes the first lawn at 150 moments in each of the four directions with the switch each way: the mower comes to rest at most 14 sixteenths of a pixel apart (`test/kernel.test.js`).
- Checked in real browsers: no scroll in either direction, a canvas of the NES's proportions, no control outside the window and the three option buttons clear of the status bar and of the touch controls at 13 window sizes from 320 by 568 to 1280 by 800 (phones in both orientations, a nearly square window and wide ones), in English and in Italian, with the information panel open; games played with real keyboard events (the cheat, START, the boost, a level cleared and its message) in a window that was shown.
- Joined the Libre Arcade collection: the page at the root of `public/`, `npm test` runs `node --test`, and the collection scaffolding was added (`scripts/serve.mjs`, `scripts/build.mjs`, an ESLint config, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, the GoatCounter snippet, the "Play here" link, the provenance, archaeology and port-map documents, the story).
- Kept the package's reports, readme, changelog and two scripts, unmodified, in `docs/development/`, with a note on what they are.

**Known limits.** The comparison does not cover the few lines of the picture on which the original changes a register in the middle of the line (33 on the playing screens, for example), the colours of the screen (a table chosen for this port), how the sound player's bytes become Web Audio, or the page's own controls and panel. The competition ROM was not compared with the competition's own file (the Internet Archive's zip holds the v1.12 ROM, not this one). The page does not model the one set-up that lasts a frame longer in the author's v1.12 ROM. The manual and the label were not read for their licence and are not kept. The exact output of the author's assembler for the source has not been reproduced. The port is NTSC only. See `PROVENANCE.md`.
