# Provenance

## What this is

Lawn Mower is a game for the Nintendo Entertainment System made by Shiru in May 2011 for the NintendoAge NES Coding Competition 2011, in 6502 assembly (NESASM) with his FamiTone sound library: a 24 KiB cartridge (mapper 0) in which the ten levels are packed in the graphics memory. You drive a mower, cut all the grass before the fuel runs out, fetch the fuel cans that appear on cut grass, and pay in fuel for flowers and stones. This folder is a port to the browser, in JavaScript, of the later revision of the source (v1.12, 1 July 2011), checked against the author's own competition ROM (v1.1, 30 May 2011) and against his own build of v1.12.

It started from a restoration package, `Lawn_Mower_M9_RC6`, that carried a JavaScript port written from the text of the source and from the package's own readings of it, its audit reports, its tests, and the two archives (the competition's and the source's). The package's tests compared the port with its own earlier output and with pictures its own scripts drew; none ran the original. What was done here is the comparison with the original, and the fixes it made necessary.

## The licence

The original is in the Public Domain in the author's words (`game.asm` line 4 and `famitone.asm` line 4, both read in the files), which the Internet Archive item's CC0 licence URL and the NESdev forum's list of homebrew that may be reproduced and sold repeat. The code of this port is GPL-3.0-or-later. Details and the exact quotations are in `reference/source-v1.12/SOURCE.md` and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`. The manual and the label of the competition archive carry wording of their own that could not be read here, and are not redistributed.

## How the port was checked

The executable oracle (`tools/oracle.mjs`, `tools/lockstep.mjs`; how it works is in `specs/port-map.md`) runs the competition ROM on a minimal NES and the port next to it, and compares at every NMI the game's variables, the picture unit's memories, the sound player's output and the picture. It runs against three programs: **the competition ROM v1.1**, the author's own executable; **the author's own v1.12 ROM** (`reference/release-v1.12/`, from the Internet Archive's zip), which is the build of the version the page follows; and **the v1.12 source assembled here**, which is what the port was written from and which behaves like the author's v1.12 ROM, although it is laid out differently, except for the length of one set-up (below). v1.1 differs from the other two in one thing that the game does, and the port has a switch for it, so that it can follow either exactly.

The last long run (`npm run test:live`, 12 October 2026, seeds 1 and 2, each program, the five scenarios and the sound sweep) found no difference at any frame. Against the competition ROM: 455,295 frames in ten runs, 526 screens set up, 455,276 pictures compared. Against the v1.12 source assembled here: 455,049 frames, 551 screens, 455,032 pictures. Against the author's own v1.12 ROM: 455,176 frames, 537 screens, 455,157 pictures, with the one exception below. The sound sweep (five tunes heard past their loops, every effect started) agreed on all three.

The exception: in the author's v1.12 ROM, loading the fifth lawn keeps the display off for 10 NMIs where the port's table (and the other two programs) say 9, on some entries: after the fourth lawn is cut (10 in four of the six games tried with the cheat player, 9 in the other two) and on some of the entries from the tank running dry on the fifth. For this program the comparison lets the port take the ROM's length for each set-up and reports the lengths that differ; nothing else differed. The page keeps the table (see "Open points").

A second pass used seeds 101, 102 and 103, which were not used while the port was written, in the short lengths of `npm test` (the picture compared every fourth frame), against the competition ROM and the assembled source only: 225,199 frames in 30 runs (15 per program), no difference at any frame. In three runs of each program the player stopped before it had reached every milestone that the short length is meant to cover (a pause, an empty tank, the final screen); `tools/live.mjs` marks those "short", and they say that the run was short, not that the ROM differed. Those milestones are checked in the short runs with seeds 1 and 2 only, which is what `npm test` runs.

`npm test` runs the same scenarios shorter, with the picture compared every fourth frame, in two to four minutes (125 and 241 seconds on the same machine in two runs, with the three programs).

### The oracle is not blind

`test/oracle-sensitivity.test.js` breaks the port thirteen ways, one at a time (a bit of the random generator, the pacing, the pad's edge rule, a segment of the fuel bar, a small tile in the picture unit's memory, one entry of the fade table, one note of the music, the animation of the mower's sprites, a blank tile of the message, the flash band of the message by three lines, the title's second band, and the cheat code), and each break is seen by the comparison with the ROM, in the run of a few hundred to a few thousand frames. The same scenarios without a break pass.

### What the oracle found in the package's port

Listed in `specs/port-map.md` ("What the oracle found") and in `CHANGELOG.md`. In short: the pacing quirk of the fuel routine, the borders of the lawn, what a level starts with, the pad model, the cheat, the frame counter through the set-ups, the messages, the sprites' priority, the fade, a few places of the sound, and the end of the game. The package's own reports are kept in `docs/development/` as they were.

## Open points

- **The competition ROM against the competition's own file.** The ROM is the file of the archive supplied with the package. The Internet Archive item's `lawn_mower.zip` (751,619 bytes) was downloaded on 12 October 2026, with the integrator's permission, to compare: it does not hold this ROM. Its ROM is the author's v1.12 (`reference/release-v1.12/`), and only its `notes.txt` and one label are byte for byte the competition archive's. So no upstream copy of the v1.1 ROM has been found, and its only evidence is the supplied file and the fact that the port, written from a source a month newer, agrees with it everywhere but in the one place that is documented.
- **The set-up of the fifth lawn.** In the author's own v1.12 ROM the display stays off for 10 NMIs, or 9, while the fifth lawn is loaded: after the fourth is cut it took 10 in four of six games tried and 9 in the other two, and it varies in the same way on the entries from the tank running dry on the fifth; the competition ROM and the assembled source took 9 every time, and the port's table (`LEVEL_SETUP_FRAMES`) says 9. The time a set-up takes is the cycles of its code, which this build lays out differently, so a small difference is the likely cause; it was not measured. The effect on the page is a black screen one sixtieth of a second shorter on those entries, and the frame counter, which the game's pacing reads, one NMI behind afterwards. The port does not model it: it has no cycle counts, only the table, and a table by lawn cannot say 9 for one entry and 10 for another; setting it to 10 would trade one error for another. A replay of three of those games (the same buttons at the same moment of play, on the ROM and on the port as the page runs it) found lawn 5 a frame out of phase: the fuel falls a frame earlier or later, the median gap between the two mowers is under 0.1 px, and the peaks were 0.3, 1.6 and 6 px (the 6 px in one game, for a third of the frames, probably after a turn taken a frame apart, which a replay does not adapt to; the cause was not examined); the one fuel can that appeared did so on the same tile at the same frame.
- **The manual and the label.** Their wording on the licence could not be read (the manual's text is in embedded fonts). The statements that cover the game are the ones in `game.asm` and `famitone.asm`.
- **The author's assembler.** The exact output of NESASM 3.1 for the v1.12 source has not been reproduced (the assembler is not in the archive); what `tools/assemble.mjs` builds differs from the competition ROM in 7,638 bytes of the program (the author's cleanup between the two versions) and from the author's own v1.12 ROM in 7,013 of the 16,384 bytes of program (both start at $C000), with the version string 35 bytes earlier, so it is laid out differently; it is identical to both in the graphics. That is why the author's own v1.12 ROM is also run: the behaviour of v1.12 as the author built it is checked on it, and the assembled source is a second program.
- **A line of the picture here and there.** The oracle does not compare the few lines on which the original changes a register in the middle of the line (line 33 on the playing screens, for example). Where in the line the change falls depends on how the CPU and the picture unit were aligned.
- **Colours and sound timbre.** The colours are a table chosen for this port and the sound is synthesised from the sound player's output; neither is the console's.
