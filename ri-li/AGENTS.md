# AGENTS.md

- **The licences, and neither is to be changed.** The original (the C++ source release, its pictures, levels and sound effects) is licensed by its author under the GNU GPL **version 2 or version 3**, in the words of
  every source header ("either version 2 or version 3"; the `NEWS` of 2.0.1 says "License upgrade to 'GNU GPL Version 2 or Version 3'"). It does **not** say "or any later version": do not write "GPL-3.0-or-later" or
  "GPL-2.0-or-later" for it, anywhere. The page, the tools and the tests are **GPL-3.0-only** (the package this started from says so, and version 3 is the one the original's own `COPYING` and `COPYING.Music` carry).
  The music is the musician's, GPL version 3 (`COPYING.Music` in the source archive). `LICENSE` is the full GPL v3 text. The sound effects have no author in the source release; do not invent one.
- **`reference/` is the author's, untouched.** `reference/Ri-li-2.0.1.tar.bz2` is checked by SHA-256 in `tools/oracle/build.sh` and in `test/reference.test.mjs`; `.gitattributes` keeps it (and `docs/development/package/`) byte for byte whatever
  `core.autocrlf` says. Do not unpack it into the repository: the data in it is 135 MB. `reference/SOURCE.md` says what is and is not known about where it came from.
- **The original is the specification, and the oracle is how it is read.** `tools/oracle/harness.cc` runs the original's own C++ (compiled unchanged, but for three tests of a pointer against 0 that C++ no longer accepts) on a virtual clock
  with scripted keys, on the real SDL 1.2.15 with its "dummy" video driver, and prints the state, the random numbers, the sounds, the list of what was drawn and the picture after each frame. `node tools/oracle.mjs check` plays the same
  scenarios on the page's own code (`tools/js-run.mjs`) and compares; `node tools/oracle.mjs menus` does it for the menus, picture by picture. It needs WSL or Linux with g++ and bash; `tools/oracle/README.md` says how to build it
  (`build-sdl.sh`, then `build.sh`) and which environment variables point to it (`RILI_ORACLE`, `RILI_WSL`). **After any change to `public/src` run `npm test`** (it replays a recording of the original, without WSL), and if the behaviour could
  have changed run `node tools/oracle.mjs check` and `menus` against the original itself, **alone**: they are heavy, and a second job next to them is a reason for a timeout. After a change to the scenarios or to the harness, record again
  (`node tools/oracle.mjs record`) so that `test/fixtures/original-trace.json` follows.
- **Do not "improve" a rule, a number, a wait or a quirk.** If the oracle says the page is wrong, the page is wrong. The things that look like bugs and are the original's (the oracle shows each): the order of the four directions at a junction
  (`Touche[]`) and the C library's random numbers are never reset between levels or games; a key that is down acts in every frame, and the arrow on the board follows the player's direction while an arrow key is down; the pause after a death
  or a clear ends one frame later than its clock says (`Mort<Horloge` is tested after the frame is shown); the table of scores inserts a score in the best slot that it beats, scanning from the bottom, with a strict comparison; the menu of the
  game keeps behind it whatever the screen held two frames before (the original draws on the back of two buffers); the arguments of a call are computed from the last to the first (g++), which decides which of two `rand()` in one call is the frame and which the offset (the pieces around the quiz panel). The random numbers and the lists of what was drawn do not show such a swap: compare pictures (`node tools/oracle.mjs quiz-pictures`, `menus`, `pixels`).
- **The oracle runs the page unchanged.** `tools/js-run.mjs` copies `public/src` into a temporary folder, appends one line to the copy of `app.mjs` (`globalThis.__rili = {...}`: the session, the renderer, the loop) and runs it in Node with a
  stand-in for the DOM, the canvas (`tools/canvas-shim.mjs`, a software canvas that draws what the renderer uses and nothing else) and the images. If you rename one of those names, or move the state out of `app.mjs`'s module scope, the
  oracle stops: change both together. Never add the hook to `public/`. The page's clock starts at 1000 as the original's does; the page steps the game by 16 ms, the length of the original's frame.
- **The page's own seed.** `globalThis.__RILI_SEED__` seeds the page's random numbers when it is set (the oracle does), and otherwise the time does; the original seeds with `srand(time)`. One generator serves the whole visit, as in the original.
- **The pictures.** The sprites are compared with the original's data pixel for pixel (`tools/check-assets.mjs`, which needs the unpacked `data/` folder: `RILI_DATA=... npm test` or `node tools/check-assets.mjs <folder>`). A browser rounds the blending of a
  translucent sprite a little differently from SDL: up to 3 of 255 in a channel, on a handful of pixels, is that, and is the tolerance of every comparison (`tools/oracle.mjs`, `comparePictures`).
- **The languages.** The page has the original's 19 (`public/src/languages.mjs`, in the order of `language.dat`: the codes are the ends of the names of the files `language.De`, `language.En`, ...). The 79 pictures of a language are in `public/ui/<code>/` and the label of its name in
  `public/ui/lang/<code>.png`; English and Italian are the package's files, the other 17 were written by `tools/extract-languages.mjs` from the original's own data (`node tools/extract-languages.mjs <the unpacked data folder>`), and `tools/check-assets.mjs` compares all of them with it pixel for pixel
  (it needs the whole `data` folder). The pictures of a language are loaded when it is chosen. The page's own texts (the bar, the guide, the credits, the status line) are in Italian and English only, and in English with the other 17: a new translation of them goes into `langText` and `infoMarkup` in `app.mjs`. The first visit starts in Italian, as the package did.
- **The package's own tests** (`docs/development/package/test` and `tests`, 12 scripts) are run unmodified against `public/` by `test/package.test.mjs`, in a temporary copy of the package's layout. Eight pass and four are expected to fail (the list, the line each
  stops at and the reasons are at the top of that file). Never edit the package's tests or documents; change the list if a later, deliberate change needs it, and say why.
- **`SHA256SUMS.txt`** lists every file of the folder (not `node_modules/`, `game/` or `.claude/`). After any change, `node scripts/checksums.mjs` rewrites it, and `node scripts/checksums.mjs --check` says what differs without changing anything.
- **`docs/development/CHANGES.md`** has a table of the SHA-256 of each file of the package's page before and now. If you change `index.html`, `styles.css` or a file of `src/`, update the table: `node scripts/changes-table.mjs --write`.
- **Player-visible text has no development vocabulary.** The guide, the credits, the status line and the buttons are in plain Italian and English. The pictures of the menus are the original's, in the language the player chose.
- **The page's layout** was measured at phone, tablet and desktop sizes; do the same after touching `public/index.html` or `public/styles.css`: no scroll, every control inside the window, the touch pad reachable. The five screenshots in `screenshots/` come from a real
  headless Chrome on the page as it is.
- `docs/development/` holds the restoration package's own notes (milestone labels, old paths) and `CHANGES.md`. Leave the notes as history; the facts of this integration are in `PROVENANCE.md`, `specs/port-map.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`.
