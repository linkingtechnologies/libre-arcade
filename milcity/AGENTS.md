# AGENTS.md

- Licence: the original is **GPL-3.0-only** (its `README` and 63 of its 66 Java files say "either version 3 of the License." and nothing about a later one; see `reference/SOURCE.md`), and so is this port. Never write "or later" for it, anywhere. If the licence is ever changed, change `package.json`, `LICENSE`, `README.md`, `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`, `PROVENANCE.md` and the SPDX headers of the code together; `test/production.test.mjs` and `test/reference.test.mjs` fail if they disagree.
- `reference/` is frozen and byte-exact (`.gitattributes` turns line-ending normalisation and text conversion off for it, because the `README` is Latin-1 with CRLF). Never reformat or add to it: `test/reference.test.mjs` fails when a kept file changes. The source archive, the deployed jar, the three pictures and the IDE's files are deliberately not kept (`reference/SOURCE.md` says why and gives their hashes). Do not put a jar, a class file, a zip or a picture of the original in `public/`: `test/production.test.mjs` fails, and the pictures have no licence of their own that anyone has established.
- **The original is the specification, and the oracle is how it is read.** `node tools/java-oracle.mjs check` (or `npm run test:live`) compiles the original's core, model and views unmodified, runs them in virtual time next to the port and compares a checksum of the state after every tenth of a second; `list` names the scenarios, `check <name>` plays only the ones named and `check --seeds N` plays each scenario N times with other seeds and the water in other steps of 5%. `JAVA` and `JAVAC` choose the tools (Java 8 or later; the original is compiled with `--release 8`). `MILCITY_CLASSES=<directory>` runs the author's deployed classes, unzipped from his jar, instead. After any change to `public/src/core/` or `public/src/ai/`, run `npm test` (the recording) and, if the behaviour of the game could have changed, `npm run test:live`. Do not "improve" a rule, a number or a quirk: if the oracle says the port is wrong, the port is wrong. If a change to the original's sources or to the harness is ever made on purpose, `test/java-oracle.test.mjs` says the recording is stale; run `npm run test:live`, read what it says, and `node tools/java-oracle.mjs record` again.
- Run the oracle **alone**. It starts a JVM per scenario and the machine this was done on had little free memory: Node and the JVM both died under pressure more than once (`-Xmx256m` is passed). Do not run `test:live` next to a browser, a second oracle or a build.
- Things that look like bugs and are the original's (the oracle shows each; `specs/port-map.md` has the others): a building hit while it is under construction shows its flame when it is finished; a hit on a cell that was destroyed scores again; the training game ends when the person loses; the computer never scores, because the score goes only to the local player; the computer plays its turn in the same pass in which it wins or loses; each player decides its own victory in its own pass; a shielded cell is the cell's type negated, so nothing can be built under a shield; a building finished under a shield drops it; explosions and the radar's fading move only when a picture is drawn, ten times a second (`step` in `m6-renderer.js`); the handset vibrates for the weapon's area in tenths of a second when the person fires, even if the shot does not come off.
- Time is the game's clock in milliseconds, `Game.advance(ms)`, with a pass at 0, 1000, 2000 ms. A building's time, a weapon's reload, a shield and the two minutes without energy are differences of that clock at the moment something happens. Do not move the game in whole seconds or settle accounts "once per tick": that was the package's first mistake.
- Randomness: the original takes a new unseeded `java.util.Random` for each coastline walker and for each choice of the computer. The port does the same through `Game.fresh()`, which takes its seeds from the browser (`browserSeeds()`) or, for the oracle and the tests, from SplitMix64 (`splitMix64(seed)`). Never fix a seed in the page: the package did, and every game had the same coast and the same computer. The number of seeds a game takes is compared too, so adding or removing a draw changes everything after it.
- The **hidden key combination** (previous building, previous mode and next mode held together: A, C and D on the handset, Q, Z and X here) sets every building's cost to zero for both players until the page is reloaded, as the original's static `Config` does. It is in the code and in the README, not in the page's instructions; `test/production.test.mjs` checks that.
- Player-visible text carries no development vocabulary (`test/production.test.mjs` checks the page and the shell). The notes for developers are in the documents of the repository, not in the page. The credits are in `app.js` in English and Italian.
- The page (`app.js`, `m5-model.js`, `index.html`) is compared with nothing: `M5Controller` is the package's design of a cursor for a mouse, not the original's `MilCityModel`. The page's timers advance the game every 50 ms (at most 250 ms at a time) and draw every 100 ms; the game stops while a dialog is open, while the page is hidden and when the person leaves. Check by measuring, in a browser, after touching `public/index.html` or `app.js`: the page was measured in English and Italian on the menu, in a game, with a building's detail open and on the instructions at 320 by 568, 360 by 640, 390 by 844, 568 by 320, 812 by 375, 1024 by 600, 1280 by 800, 700 by 700, 900 by 700, 600 by 700 and 1100 by 800, with no scroll, every control outside the panel inside the window and the panel scrolling inside itself. The panel came out of the window at 568 by 320 in the package's page; the fix is the last media query of `index.html` and `shortLandscape` in `updatePhoneScale`. Also look for content that spills out of its box inside the panel (compare each descendant's bottom with its block's): the card of the selected action was squeezed by its `min-height` when the panel was low, and `flex:none` on `.actionCard` is what stops it. Every child of `#gamePanel` that has a `min-height` smaller than its content needs the same.
- Do not draw text in the canvas. The canvas is 480 px wide and is shown at about 430 (down to about 130) with `image-rendering: pixelated`, nearest neighbour at a ratio that is not a whole number, which drops rows of pixels and cuts letters. The status text under the map and the game-over banner are HTML elements laid over the canvas (`#hud`, `#hudBanner`, `setHud` and `setBanner` in `app.js`). The tiles, which are blocks of colour, are fine with the scaling.
- `public/src/ui/m6-renderer.js` draws the tiles and the splash from rectangles. They imitate the original's art on purpose and the licence of that art is not established (`PROVENANCE.md`, open points). Do not copy pixels from the original's pictures into the page, and do not add the pictures.
- The Bluetooth game (`reference/.../bluetooth/`, `RemotePlayer`) is not ported and not compiled by the oracle.
- `docs/development/` holds the restoration package's own notes with its milestone labels and old paths, and `CHANGES.md`, the list of what was changed in its JavaScript. Leave them as history; the facts of this integration are in `PROVENANCE.md`, `specs/port-map.md` and `CHANGELOG.md`. Some of them say things that the comparison with the original showed to be untrue (the browser being "source-parity certified" among them); `docs/development/README.md` says so.
- `test/java-oracle/` holds this repository's own stand-ins for the handset's API and for the clock, the threads and the random numbers. They contain nothing from any vendor's MIDP and are GPL-3.0-only like the rest. Windows writes `\r\n` in the original's output: split on `/\r?\n/`.
