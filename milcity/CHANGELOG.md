# Changelog

## 1.0.0

First release in Libre Arcade, promoted from the restoration package `milcity-libre-arcade-1.0.0-archaeology.zip` after a comparison with the original game, run in virtual time next to the port, and a check in real browsers.

- A real-time strategy game of cities and weapons for one player against the computer: the nine buildings, the six weapons, the economy and the energy, the shields and the radar, the four levels of the computer and the training game, the random 30 by 30 map and the score limit of MilCity - Blue Edition (Jérémy Walther, 2011, for J2ME phones), ported from its Java 1.0.5 source. English and Italian, keyboard, mouse and touch, the options of the original (water, score limit, starting money) and its vibration option, an unfinished game kept in the browser and resumed from the menu. Its hidden key combination is in it (see the README).
- Preserved the original under `reference/`: the 66 Java files of the source, `COPYING`, `README` and `Application Descriptor`, byte for byte, with a checksum list, the list of the 81 files of the archive they came from, and a record of where the archive comes from, what is known about it and what is not, its licence and the evidence for it, and what is not kept (the deployed jar, the three pictures and the IDE's project files, with their hashes; `reference/SOURCE.md`).
- Added an executable oracle (`test/java-oracle/`, `tools/`): the original's core, model, views and engine `paint()` compiled unmodified and run on a Java 8 or later with stand-ins for the handset's API and a class loader that changes three class names (`Thread`, `System`, `Random`) to a virtual-time thread, a virtual clock and a generator fed with given seeds; a cooperative scheduler for the two players' threads and the engine's; and a harness that plays a scenario on the original and on the port and compares a checksum of the state after each tenth of a second, printing the first line of the state that differs. The port was compared in 65 scenarios (37,800 seconds of game, 7,308 actions, 10,169 seeds) and agreed in all, and the original's own deployed classes agreed in 39 more (22,680 seconds). `npm test` replays a recording of 13 of them without Java; `npm run test:live` plays them against the original. See `specs/port-map.md` and `PROVENANCE.md`.
- Fixed what the comparison showed to differ from the original, in the package's port:
  - the time: the game moves in milliseconds and the players settle their accounts at the moment the game starts and every second after it, while what the person does happens at any moment (a building's time of making and a weapon's reload are read from the clock when you act);
  - the computer: it chooses its opponent with a generator of its own, as the original does, so every later choice and number used is the original's; it plays its turn in the pass in which it wins or loses; it never scores, because the original gives points only to the local player;
  - the end: each player decides on its own victory in its own pass, and the training game ends when the person loses;
  - the map: a new generator for each of the ten coastline walkers, and the round of ten is not cut short when the water reaches its target (it showed with the water at 45% or 55%);
  - buildings and shields: a building hit while it is under construction shows its flame when finished; a shielded cell is the cell's type negated until the first pass after its minute, nothing can be built under a shield, and a building finished under a shield drops it;
  - the pictures: explosions and the radar's fading move ten times a second and no faster (a click used to move them);
  - the seeds: every game takes its seeds from the browser (the package fixed one);
  - the vibration: the handset's `size × 100` milliseconds for a shot, as in the original, now with an option to switch it off.
- Changed the page where the way it is put together here called for it: the game's clock follows real time in steps of 50 ms, at most 250 ms at a time; the game stops while a dialog is open, while the page is hidden and when the person leaves; the options are kept in the browser; the credits are written for a player; the page counts visits with the collection's counter; and a window wider than it is tall and at most 420 px high (a phone held sideways, 568 by 320) puts the picture and the panel side by side, where the package's page let the panel come out of the window by 31 px; the status text under the map and the game-over banner are HTML over the canvas, because drawn in it they were cut at the bottom by the scaling of the canvas, and the card of the selected building or weapon is no longer squeezed when the panel is low, which had let its last line ("Under construction: about 13s.") come out of the box (two reader's reports, then measured).
- Added tests: the numbers of the game against the original's source (`test/core.test.mjs`); the replay of the original's recording (`test/java-oracle.test.mjs`); nine deliberate breaks of the port that the replay must see (`test/oracle-sensitivity.test.mjs`); the preserved files, the licence and the record (`test/reference.test.mjs`); and the deployed tree, the page's imports and what a player reads (`test/production.test.mjs`). 43 tests. The package's tests, which compared the port with what its authors had read and with itself, were not kept.
- Checked in real browsers (Chromium-based): a game played from the menu through building, the computer's attacks, the military view, a reload and resuming, the options, the language and the hidden key combination, and the page's layout measured at eleven window sizes in English and Italian (no scroll, every control inside the window); see `PROVENANCE.md`.
- Joined the Libre Arcade collection: the page at the root of `public/`, `npm test` runs `node --test`, and the collection scaffolding was added (`scripts/serve.mjs`, `scripts/build.mjs`, an ESLint config, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, the GoatCounter snippet, the "Play here" link, the provenance, archaeology and port-map documents, the story).
- Kept the package's own documents, unmodified, in `docs/development/package/`, with a note on what they are and a list of what was changed in its JavaScript (`docs/development/CHANGES.md`).

**Known limits.** The comparison covers the game and not the page: the menus, the panel, the texts, the mouse and keyboard, the saving and the layout were driven in a browser and read, not compared with the original, and neither was the original's cursor model. The pictures are drawn by the page and imitate the original's, whose licence is not established, so whether they are free of it is an open point. The source archive is the file SourceForge serves (downloaded and compared by SHA-256), but the deployed jar was not compared with SourceForge's. A pause stops the game here and did not in the original. The order in which the original's threads wake at the same instant is a race on a handset and is fixed in the oracle and the port. The Bluetooth game is not ported. See `PROVENANCE.md`.
