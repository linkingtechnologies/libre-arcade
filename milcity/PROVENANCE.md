# Provenance

## What this is

MilCity - Blue Edition is a real-time strategy game for J2ME phones (CLDC 1.0, MIDP 2.0, 320 by 240 pixels) by Jérémy Walther, published on SourceForge in 2011; the version ported is 1.0.5 of 19 February 2011. Each player builds a city on a 30 by 30 map, earns money, produces energy and weapons, and shoots at the other's city as in Battleship. The computer plays at four levels; there is also a Bluetooth game for two phones, which is not ported. The Java package of the source is `fr.uha.ensisa.ir.walther.milcityblue`; nothing in the files says more about who the author was or why he wrote it.

This folder is a port to the browser, in JavaScript, of the game's rules, its computer player and its map, with a new page around them. It started from a restoration package, `milcity-libre-arcade-1.0.0-archaeology.zip`, that carried a JavaScript port written from a reading of the source, the source archive, and a great many reports. The package's tests asserted what its authors had read in the source and that the port repeats itself under a seed; none ran the original. What was done here is the comparison with the original, run, and the fixes it made necessary.

## The licence

The original is **GPL-3.0-only**: its `README` and 63 of its 66 Java files say "either version 3 of the License." and stop there, without "or (at your option) any later version"; the other three files have no notice. The full text of version 3 is `COPYING` in the archive and `LICENSE` here. Nothing grants a later version, so this port, derived from that code, is GPL-3.0-only too; `test/reference.test.mjs` and `test/production.test.mjs` fail if either is changed. The exact words and the evidence are in `reference/SOURCE.md`, and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` repeats them.

The three pictures of the original (`res/images.png`, `res/MilCityLogo_Shiro_icon.png` and `res/Splash_MilCity_Koojoo.png`) are inside the GPL project and say nothing of their own about their licence; two have names that credit "Shiro" and "Koojoo", and no one has established who they are. They are not kept here and the page does not load them.

## How it was checked

**The oracle.** The original's classes (the game's core, the model, the views and the `paint()` of the engine) are compiled unmodified and run in virtual time next to the port, and a checksum of the state of both is compared after each tenth of a second. A scenario sets the options and a seed and lists what the person does in each tenth of a second. How it works, what it stands in for and what it assumes is in `specs/port-map.md`. The pieces that are not the original's are the stand-ins for the handset's API, the virtual clock and threads, the order in which two threads wake at the same instant (a race on a handset) and the sequence of seeds the original is given for its generators (a generator of the original is seeded from the clock; the oracle gives both sides the same seeds).

**What was run.**

- 13 scenarios, between one and fifteen minutes of game, are the recording `test/fixtures/java-oracle.json`; `npm test` replays them against the port without Java.
- A wider run, `node tools/java-oracle.mjs check --seeds 5`, played the 13 scenarios with five different seeds each: **65 scenarios, 378,000 tenths of a second (37,800 seconds of game), 7,308 actions of the person, and 10,169 random seeds taken by the original, and the port agreed with the original in every one**.
- The same, on the classes the author compiled and deployed (the `MilCity-Blue.jar` of the archive, unzipped and given to the oracle with `MILCITY_CLASSES`; compiled for class file version 46): `--seeds 3`, **39 scenarios, 226,800 tenths of a second (22,680 seconds of game), 3,067 actions, 5,364 seeds, all in agreement**. That the classes compiled here and the author's behave alike in these scenarios is the evidence that compiling them again did not change the game.
- `test/oracle-sensitivity.test.mjs` breaks the port nine ways (a missile silo costing 11, a shield covering a larger area, the two minutes without energy a second shorter, the vibration half as long, the computer choosing its target with one random number fewer, and four faults of timing and of who decides the end) and requires the replay to see each one, so the comparison is known to be able to fail.

**What the scenarios reach.** `node tools/oracle-coverage.mjs` plays them on the port and counts what happened. In the 13 scenarios together: 120 missile silos, 105 power plants, 149 commercial buildings, 45 air fields, 37 nuke silos, 25 factories, 23 houses, 21 shield generators and 20 laboratories were built; 408 missiles, 101 aircraft, 68 nukes, 63 shields, 48 mortars and 29 spies were used; 145 shields ran out, 156 buildings were hit, 133 destroyed, and 798 cells became craters. The games end in the ways the original has: won by the person, won by the computer, ended with no winner (a blackout, a game left, a training game lost) and still running at the end of the scenario. A scenario is not a proof of what it does not reach, and the limits are listed in `specs/port-map.md`.

**Faults of the oracle itself,** found and fixed on the way: the engine's thread measured a picture with the real clock instead of the virtual one (the pictures drifted against the passes); a check whose option had been mistyped selected no scenario and reported success (it now fails); and the original's output, written on Windows with a carriage return before each line break, was compared with the port's without taking that into account.

**Level of confidence** (the collection's levels are in the root `AGENTS.md`). Level 1, an executable oracle, for the game: economy, buildings, weapons, shields, radar, the computer, the map, the end, the score and the vibration. Level 3, reading and trying it in a browser, for the page. No claim is made beyond that: the port is not "certified", and the package's claim that it was "source-parity certified" (`docs/development/package/M8_PARITY_CERTIFICATION.md`) is the package's, made without running the original.

**What the oracle found in the package's port** is listed in `specs/port-map.md` (12 differences: the clock and the passes, the computer's choice of target, the coastline, a building hit while it is built, the training game's end, who scores, the computer's last turn, who decides on victory, the shields, the pictures moving the game, the fixed seed and the vibration), and each was fixed in `public/src/`.

**The page.** It was driven in two real browsers, both Chromium-based (no Firefox or Safari), with real clicks and key events: the desktop app's browser pane (a game started, a power plant and a commercial building built and finished, the hidden key combination, a silo built under attack by the computer, the Military view, a reload and "Resume Game", the options changed and found again in `localStorage`, Italian, the credits, and the game's clock found to stand still for the six seconds the page was made to look hidden) and a headless Chrome (an Easy game with 18 buildings of all nine kinds, 30 clicks on the opponent's map with a missile ready for the first, English and Italian, and the phone size 390 by 844, driven by synthetic clicks on the page's map). The layout was measured in each of English and Italian, on the menu, in a game, with a building's detail open and on the instructions, at 320 by 568, 360 by 640, 390 by 844, 568 by 320, 812 by 375, 1024 by 600, 1280 by 800, 700 by 700, 900 by 700, 600 by 700 and 1100 by 800: the page never scrolls, every control that is not in the panel is inside the window and the panel scrolls inside itself when it must. One defect was found (the panel came out of the window by 31 px at 568 by 320) and fixed (`docs/development/CHANGES.md`). No error reached the console. None of this compares the page with the original.

## Open points

1. **The deployed jar was not compared with SourceForge's.** The source archive was (it is the same file, `reference/SOURCE.md`); the jar of the archive (156,464 bytes) agrees with the size SourceForge lists, but the jar SourceForge serves was not downloaded and hashed. The oracle ran the jar's classes from the archive, not from SourceForge.
2. **The art.** The page draws its own tiles and splash, but they imitate the original's: the package measured the colours of the tile sheet and drew each tile "in the same vocabulary", and the splash repeats the composition of the original's (two large kanji, 攻城, over the word *milcity* with *blue* under it). That a drawing made after art whose licence is unknown is free of it is not something the evidence settles. The integrator decided, on 12 October 2026, to leave the page as it is. If it ever matters, the tiles and the splash are in `public/src/ui/m6-renderer.js` and `public/src/ui/app.js` (`showSplash`) and can be replaced without touching the game.
3. **The cursor and the page are not compared** with the original's `MilCityModel` and `MilCityBlue` (level 3). The original moves a cursor with the handset's keys; the page is designed for a mouse and a keyboard.
4. **A pause stops the game here and not in the original,** where the timers read the phone's clock. This is deliberate and listed in `specs/port-map.md`, and the integrator confirmed it on 12 October 2026.
5. **Training mode** has no enemy radar in the page; the original lets the person shoot at their own city.
6. **The Bluetooth game** is not ported, on purpose: it needs two phones, and the author's own changelog says it did not work in the first release. The integrator confirmed on 12 October 2026 that it is not to be ported.
7. **The order of two threads at the same instant** is a race on a handset; the oracle and the port fix it (the person's, then the computer's). Games where the original's order would have differed are not covered.
8. **The author's address** appears in three forms in the archive (`reference/SOURCE.md`); none is used by the page.
