# Changelog

## 1.0.0

First release in Libre Arcade, promoted from the restoration package `zooming-secretary-1.0.0-rc9-m32-source.zip` after a comparison with the original ROM, run next to the port on a small NES of our own, and a check in a real browser.

- A game of telephones and filing cabinets for one player: the eight stages (Days 1 to 7 and the Dream), the seven characters (Chief, Bouncer, Chatter, Geek, ManBox, Dibrov and the Dream's Ghost), the coffee, the wisdom, the allowance of missed calls and the Dream's bonus, of Zooming Secretary v1.02 (NES, 2011) by Shiru and Alexei "PinWizz" Bespalko
- Recorded where the original comes from (`reference/`): a record with the SHA-256 of the ROM, of the author's notes and of the eight source files that were read, what is known about them and what is not. No byte of the original is kept
- Added an executable oracle (`tools/`): the ROM of v1.02 run on a small NES written for the purpose (a 6502 core and an NROM board, copied from Lawn Mower's), with the same buttons given to the port, and about ninety values of the state compared after every iteration of the game's main loop. The ROM's variables are found from the order in which the compiler laid them out, anchored on the one array that can be seen in the running program
- Added scripted players for it (one that presses nothing, one that wanders, one that answers the calls by planning a path with the port's own movement code), and campaigns that let the ROM go through its own screens between stages, so that the allowance, the stage that follows and the random generator's byte at the start of each are the ROM's
- Fixed what the comparison showed to differ from the original, in the package's port:
  - the ladders: a climbing step that has just run out looks for the next one in the same iteration, the ladder flag stays set until a direction is pressed with no ladder to take, and the direction is kept when nothing is pressed;
  - the left edge: ManBox and Dibrov take their position as an unsigned byte to decide whether they touch the player;
  - the hearts: they take two numbers from the random generator only when one of the eight slots is free (new `public/src/hearts.js`);
  - the Chief: left of the 32nd column he never slows the player, because the original compares the difference as an unsigned number;
  - the seeds (the page): each stage starts from the byte the one before ended with, the first from the title screen, and not from 1.
- Changed the page where the way it is put together here called for it: the credits are in plain language in English and Italian, there is an empty favicon link, and the collection's visit counter is before `</body>`
- Added what the original has and the package had not ported, written from the source and compared with the ROM: its pause (START: the game stops, and the program lets START end the pause only after half a second and begin another after half a second more), the hidden code on the title screen (B, A, B, A, left, up, B, A) that lets the player choose the level before every stage, and the sound test (A, B, SELECT and START held together). The page has them for the keyboard (Z and X are A and B, Shift SELECT, Enter and Space START), does not mention them, and the sound test plays its own sounds (no music)
- Made the cards of the page (the days' introductions, the results, the pause) fit without scrolling inside themselves in every window that was measured, including a phone upright, where the button used to be out of sight below the text
- Added tests: the recording of the ROM replayed against the port (`test/rom-oracle.test.mjs`, 104 scenarios, 47 front ends and 5 campaigns, with 19 deliberate breaks of the port that the replay must see); the package's own 24 test runners, run unmodified against `public/` in a temporary copy of its layout, with the thirteen cases that pin what was corrected or replaced listed and explained (`test/package.test.mjs`); the kept record, the licences and the page's strings (`test/reference.test.mjs`, `test/production.test.mjs`)
- Checked in a real browser (Chromium-based): the whole campaign played by a bot in real time, from the title to the ending (Days 1 to 4, the Dream lost, Day 5 lost once and then won, Days 6 and 7), the pause, the sound, the language, the credits and the preferences kept in the browser, and the page's layout measured at ten window sizes in English and Italian, and at four short landscape sizes with touch emulation
- Joined the Libre Arcade collection: the page at the root of `public/`, `npm test` runs `node --test`, and the collection scaffolding was added (`scripts/serve.mjs`, `scripts/build.mjs`, an ESLint config, `.gitignore`, `.gitattributes`, the GoatCounter snippet, the "Play here" link, the provenance, the port map, the notices, the story)
- Kept the package's own documents, unmodified, in `docs/development/package/`, with a note on what they are and a list of what was changed in its JavaScript (`docs/development/CHANGES.md`)

The last long run compared 484,895 iterations of the game (9,698 seconds) in 104 scenarios (48 of them with the bots pressing START) and 5 campaigns, and 14,082 passes of the front end in 47 front ends, and found no difference. `npm test` passes 230 tests.

**Known limits.** The comparison covers the rules and the campaign, not the page: the pictures, the sounds, the texts, the touch controls and the layout were driven in a browser and read, not compared with the original, and only in browsers based on Chromium. The sound test plays the page's own sounds, not the original's 21 effects and 7 tunes, and has no music; the hidden code, the sound test and the choice of the level need a keyboard. Answering a call by wisdom before any cabinet is not covered. The files used are third parties' copies of the author's release (an emulator project's repository and the Internet Archive), which agree with each other and with the package's own record of the release archive; that they are the authors' bytes is not established. The release's manual and label carry "CC BY 2011" and say "Creative Commons Attribution license", and the version of that grant is not stated anywhere that was read. See [`PROVENANCE.md`](PROVENANCE.md).
